# Referee Report: Reviewer 1 (Methodology & Bayesian Causal Inference)

**Manuscript Title:** *Does Increasing Government Spend on Tertiary Education Improve the Future Tax Take? A Bayesian Causal Inference Analysis*  
**Author:** Andrew C. Parnell  
**Date of Review:** 5 November 2026

### 1. Overall impression

This manuscript investigates an important and policy-relevant macroeconomic question: whether increasing public expenditure per tertiary student strengthens a country's future tax capacity and shifts revenue composition toward direct income, profit, and capital gains taxation once post-treatment macroeconomic growth is netted out. The paper has several notable strengths. The conceptual framing around the UNESCO per-student denominator trap is sharp and well-motivated, the joint disaggregation of tax revenue into four pure percentage-point channels (including indirect goods and services taxation as a negative-control outcome) avoids mechanical GDP product identities, the references in `references.bib` are verified and appropriate, and the C++ (`RcppArmadillo`) and R pipeline is well-organised and reproducible.

Despite these strengths, a rigorous audit of the mathematical formulations, C++ MCMC samplers (`src/cpp/mvbcf_source_code.cpp` and `src/cpp/lbcf_3wave.cpp`), and R estimation scripts (`src/r/02_build_clean_tax_panel.R`, `src/r/04_run_mvbcf_tax_models.R`, `src/r/05_run_longitudinal_bcf_tax.R`, and `src/r/06_generate_figures_and_diagnostics.R`) uncovers several serious methodological, algorithmic, and econometric flaws that currently stand as barriers to publication. Most critically: (1) the three-wave longitudinal BCF (`lbcf_3wave`) leaks $+10$-year ($t+2$) future mediators into the $+5$-year ($t+1$) growth forest while imposing a homoskedastic i.i.d. error structure across stacked fiscal levels and changes that causes the $+5$-year Direct Income Tax / GDP effect to flip sign relative to `mvbcf`; (2) the "country-clustered" Dirichlet block-bootstrap only reweights unit-level CATE means post-hoc and does nothing to correct the i.i.d. likelihood for within-country serial correlation or the mechanical moving-average overlap of $+10$-year changes across five-year waves; (3) the single-stage inclusion of post-treatment mediators inside the prognostic forest $\mu(\cdot)$ in Model `M2` ignores intermediate collider bias and treatment-mediator interactions; (4) the C++ Metropolis-Hastings tree proposals omit the Chipman et al. (1998, 2010) terminal-to-nog node transition ratio, and several primary and robustness chains exhibit severe Geweke non-stationarity ($|z| > 3$, reaching $-8.30$) and low effective sample size ($\text{ESS} = 58$); and (5) the benchmark comparison omits standard causal machine-learning estimators (`GRF`, Double-ML `Lasso` and `RF`, and true Two-Way Fixed Effects) and compares `mvbcf` against univariate BCFs fitted on different sample sizes. My recommendation is **major revision** (bordering on reject and resubmit unless the C++ and R specifications are corrected and re-estimated).

### 2. Major concerns

1. **Future-mediator look-ahead leakage, omitted mediators, and misspecified i.i.d. level-change variance in the Three-Wave Longitudinal BCF (`lbcf_3wave`).**  
   In `src/r/05_run_longitudinal_bcf_tax.R` (lines 94 - 98, 123, and 189 - 190), the covariate matrix `X_growth_stack` is constructed from `growth_vars_med`, which stacks both $+5$-year ($t+1$) and $+10$-year ($t+2$) forward mediators (`d_t1_log_gdp_pc_pct`, `d_t2_log_gdp_pc_pct`, `d_t1_tert_enrol_pp`, `d_t2_tert_enrol_pp`), and is passed identically into **both** the Wave 1 growth forest $\mu_1$ (`X_growth`) and the Wave 2 growth forest $\mu_2$ (`X_growth2`). Consequently, the $+5$-year growth forest $\mu_1$ splits on future macroeconomic changes over $[t+1, t+2]$ that have not yet occurred at $+5$ years ($t+1$), violating temporal causality. Simultaneously, `05_run_longitudinal_bcf_tax.R` omits two of the four mediators used in `mvbcf` Model `M2` (forward Gini inequality change `d_t1_gini_disp` / `d_t2_gini_disp` and forward life expectancy change `d_t1_life_exp_yrs` / `d_t2_life_exp_yrs`).  
   Furthermore, in Appendix A Equation (4) (`paper.tex` line 247) and `src/cpp/lbcf_3wave.cpp` (line 1066), `fast_lbcf3` stacks the baseline fiscal level $Y_{i,0}$ and future levels $Y_{i,1} = Y_{i,0} + \Delta Y_{i,t+1}$ and $Y_{i,2} = Y_{i,0} + \Delta Y_{i,t+2}$ into a $3N = 846$ vector and samples a single homoskedastic residual precision $\tau = \sigma^{-2}$ across all three waves while excluding $Y_{i,0}$ from $\mu_0(X_{i,t})$. Because $\mu_0(X_{i,t})$ only explains a fraction of cross-country baseline level differences, the unmodelled baseline residual $u_{i,0} = Y_{i,0} - \mu_0(X_{i,t})$ is mechanically carried forward into $Y_{i,1}$ and $Y_{i,2}$. This violates the i.i.d. residual assumption, inflates the effective sample size in `sample_tau` by treating three observations on the same trajectory as independent, and contaminates the within-country change variance with cross-country level variance. This misspecification explains the glaring contradiction between Table 2 (`mvbcf`) and Table 5 (`lbcf`) on **Direct Income Tax / GDP**: in `mvbcf`, the $+5$-year direct effect is $+0.20\text{ pp}$ ($P = 0.94$) and $+10$-year is $+0.28\text{ pp}$ ($P = 0.92$), whereas in `lbcf` the $+5$-year effect ($\tau_1$) flips sign to $-0.06\text{ pp}$ ($P = 0.36$) and the cumulative 10-year effect drops to $+0.14\text{ pp}$ ($P = 0.79$), while the negative-control Goods Tax Share jumps to $+0.43\text{ pp}$ ($P = 0.74$) at $+5$ years.  
   *Actionable fix:* In `05_run_longitudinal_bcf_tax.R`, pass strictly Wave-1 mediators (`d_t1_*`, including Gini and life expectancy) to `X_growth` and Wave-2 mediators (`d_t2_*`) to `X_growth2`. More importantly, either include a trajectory-specific random intercept $\alpha_i$ (or subtract $Y_{i,0}$ so `lbcf_3wave` models stacked cumulative changes $(0, \Delta Y_{i,t+1}, \Delta Y_{i,t+2})$ with wave-specific residual variances $\sigma_w^2$) so that unmodelled cross-country baseline levels $Y_{i,0} - \mu_0(X_{i,t})$ cannot bias $\tau_1$ and $\tau_2$, and explicitly discuss the reconciliation between `mvbcf` and `lbcf` on Outcome 2 in Section 5.3.

2. **Causal identification of Model `M2`: Intermediate collider bias and unstated Controlled vs Natural Direct Effect assumptions under sequential ignorability.**  
   Section 4.2 defines the Mediator-Removed Direct Effect (`M2`) by appending four contemporaneous post-treatment changes $M_{i,t+h}$ (measured over $[t, t+h]$) directly into the prognostic forest covariate matrix $X_{\text{con}}$ alongside baseline covariates $X_{i,t}$ and the pre-treatment propensity score $\hat{\pi}(X_{i,t})$: $\Delta Y_{i,t+h} = \mu(X_{i,t}, M_{i,t+h}, \hat{\pi}(X_{i,t})) + \tau(W_{i,t}) Z_{i,t} + \varepsilon_{i,t+h}$. While Section 2.3 cites Acharya, Blackwell, and Sen (2016), Imai et al. (2010), and VanderWeele (2016), the manuscript never writes down potential outcomes $Y_{i,t+h}(z, m)$ and fails to heed the central warning of Acharya et al. (2016): simply conditioning on a post-treatment mediator $M_{i,t+h}$ in a single-stage outcome regression induces **collider (intermediate variable) bias** whenever an unobserved post-treatment macroeconomic shock $U_{i,t+h}$ (for example, a terms-of-trade boom, fiscal consolidation, or tax administration reform during $[t, t+h]$) simultaneously affects both GDP growth $M_{i,t+h}$ and tax revenue $\Delta Y_{i,t+h}$, opening the non-causal path $Z_{i,t} \to M_{i,t+h} \leftarrow U_{i,t+h} \to \Delta Y_{i,t+h}$. Even under sequential ignorability, Equation (2) restricts $M_{i,t+h}$ to enter only $\mu(\cdot)$ and excludes $M_{i,t+h}$ from the treatment-effect forest $\tau(W_{i,t})$, which implicitly imposes **zero treatment-mediator interaction** (under which the Controlled Direct Effect $\text{CDE}(m)$ equals the Natural Direct Effect $\text{NDE}$). Notice that in Table 2, the estimated direct effect (`M2`) is actually *larger* than the total effect (`M1`) for Non-Resource Tax / GDP at $+10$ years ($+0.53\text{ pp}$ vs $+0.37\text{ pp}$) and Direct Income Tax Share ($+1.66\text{ pp}$ vs $+1.38\text{ pp}$), which either implies negative indirect mediation or reflects collider bias from conditioning on post-treatment GDP growth and enrolment changes.  
   *Actionable fix:* Formally define the causal estimand (Controlled Direct Effect $\text{CDE}(m)$ vs Natural Direct Effect $\text{NDE}$) using potential outcomes $Y_{i,t+h}(z, m)$ in Section 4.2, state the two-stage sequential ignorability assumption and the no-treatment-mediator-interaction assumption explicitly, and implement a two-stage sequential g-estimation check (Acharya et al., 2016) - first estimating the effect of $M_{i,t+h}$ on $\Delta Y_{i,t+h}$ to demediate the outcome, and then fitting `mvbcf` on the demediated outcome using strictly pre-treatment covariates - to verify that single-stage post-treatment conditioning does not distort the direct effect estimates. Also add a formal sensitivity analysis (or explicit analytical bound) for post-treatment mediator-outcome confounding $\rho(\varepsilon_M, \varepsilon_Y)$.

3. **Country-clustered uncertainty quantification and overlapping 10-year moving-average panel dependence.**  
   Section 4.2 (line 200) claims that *"To account for within-country serial correlation across waves, all inference is reported alongside a country-clustered Dirichlet block-bootstrap over sovereign countries"*. Inspection of `src/r/04_run_mvbcf_tax_models.R` (lines 235 - 241 and 260 - 261) reveals that this step merely multiplies the post-hoc MCMC draws of the unit-level CATE function $\tau^{(s)}(W_{i,t})$ by country-level Dirichlet weights $w_c^{(s)}$ (`clu_draws <- colSums(tau_mat * res$obs_w)`). Because $\tau^{(s)}(W_{i,t})$ is a heavily shrunk tree ensemble ($M_\tau = 30, \alpha_\tau = 0.25, \beta_\tau = 3.0$) whose cross-unit dispersion within any single MCMC draw $s$ is tiny relative to the between-draw posterior variance of the leaf parameters, Dirichlet reweighting of $\tau^{(s)}(W_{i,t})$ only measures finite-sample covariate weighting variability in $W_{i,t}$ and leaves the posterior credible intervals virtually unchanged to the second decimal place (for example, in `table1_mvbcf_ate_posteriors.csv` Row 14, the unclustered 95% CrI is $[-0.162, +1.232]$ with $P = 0.930$, and the "clustered" CrI is $[-0.173, +1.258]$ with $P = 0.926$). Crucially, during MCMC fitting in `mvbcf_source_code.cpp` (lines 474 and 529), the likelihood assumes $\varepsilon_{i,t+h} \stackrel{\text{i.i.d.}}{\sim} \mathcal{N}_4(0, \Sigma)$. At the $+10$-year horizon ($t \to t+2$), consecutive five-year waves for the same country ($t \to t+2$ and $t+1 \to t+3$) overlap by five years ($[t+1, t+2]$), creating mechanical first-order moving-average $\text{MA}(1)$ serial correlation in $\varepsilon_{i,t+2}$ and inflating the precision $n_b \Sigma^{-1}$ in every conjugate leaf update.  
   *Actionable fix:* Revise Section 4.2 so it does not mischaracterise post-hoc Dirichlet reweighting of $\tau^{(s)}(W_{i,t})$ as correcting for likelihood-level within-country serial correlation. To properly account for within-country error clustering and overlapping 10-year horizons inside the Bayesian model, either (a) incorporate a country-level random effect vector $u_c \sim \mathcal{N}_4(0, \Sigma_u)$ in the `mvbcf` Gibbs sampler, or (b) run an outer cluster block-bootstrap (resampling the $C = 112$ countries with replacement *prior* to fitting `mvbcf`, or tempering the likelihood precision by the within-country effective sample size) and compare those intervals directly against the non-overlapping decadal wave specification (`R2`, $N = 154$).

4. **Asymmetric Metropolis-Hastings tree transition ratios in C++ samplers and severe MCMC non-stationarity ($|z_{\text{Geweke}}| > 3$, $\text{ESS} = 58$).**  
   First, in both `src/cpp/mvbcf_source_code.cpp` (lines 771 - 784 and 839 - 855) and `src/cpp/lbcf_3wave.cpp` (lines 716 - 729, 785 - 800, 857 - 872, 928 - 943, and 1000 - 1015), the Metropolis-Hastings acceptance probability for `Grow`, `Prune`, `Change`, and `Swap` proposals is computed as `a = exp(lnew - lold)`, where `log_lik()` sums the integrated marginal log-likelihood and the log tree-structure prior $\log P(T)$. However, in the standard BART algorithm (Chipman et al., 1998, 2010), `Grow` selects a terminal node uniformly from $b_{\text{term}}(T^{\text{old}})$ terminal nodes whereas `Prune` selects a doubly-terminal parent ("nog") node uniformly from $w_2(T^{\text{new}})$ nog nodes. Omitting the Hastings proposal transition ratio $\frac{b_{\text{term}}(T^{\text{old}})}{w_2(T^{\text{new}})}$ from `Grow` (and its reciprocal from `Prune`) violates detailed balance whenever $b_{\text{term}}(T^{\text{old}}) \neq w_2(T^{\text{new}})$. In addition, `Tree::swap()` (`mvbcf_source_code.cpp` lines 416 - 436) swaps a parent split rule with a single child without handling the sibling split rule or checking whether the swapped parent rule produces empty descendant leaves prior to `change_update()`.  
   Second, the MCMC convergence diagnostics reveal substantial mixing problems that are understated in the manuscript:
   - In `outputs/tables/table1_mvbcf_ate_posteriors.csv` and `table4_robustness_checks.csv`, the Geweke $z$-scores exceed $\pm 3.0$ across numerous specifications: `M1a_Total_5y` Outcome 1 ($z = -3.29$), `M1b_Total_10y` Outcome 3 ($z = +3.67$) and Outcome 4 ($z = +7.60$), `M2a_Direct_5y` Outcome 4 ($z = -4.07$), `M2b_Direct_10y` Outcomes 1 - 3 ($z = -3.88, -2.83, -2.44$), `M3d_RealPPP_10y` Outcome 1 ($z = -5.59$) and Outcome 4 ($z = -3.19$), and `M3f_TertGDP_10y` Outcome 4 ($z = -8.30$).
   - Even more troubling, `04_run_mvbcf_tax_models.R` (lines 164 - 183) and `06_generate_figures_and_diagnostics.R` (lines 548 - 580) define `calc_ess` / `compute_ess` and `calc_geweke_z` / `compute_geweke_z` using **two different formulas**, causing `table1_mvbcf_ate_posteriors.csv` and Appendix Table 9 (`tab:mcmc` / `tableA1_convergence_and_fit_diagnostics.csv`) to report conflicting ESS and Geweke numbers for the exact same chains! Under the multi-lag formula in Table 9 (`paper.tex` line 307), Outcome 8 (`Goods Tax Share` at $+10\text{y}$) has **$\text{ESS} = 58$** out of 1,250 retained draws (visible as low-frequency drift in Figure A1 Panel 4), well below the standard $\text{ESS} \ge 100\text{ to }400$ reliability threshold. Finally, $\hat{R}$ is computed by splitting a single 1,250-draw chain rather than running multiple independent chains, and zero MCMC convergence diagnostics ($\hat{R}$, ESS, or Geweke $z$) are reported for the `lbcf_3wave` models.  
   *Actionable fix:* Correct the Grow/Prune Metropolis-Hastings transition ratio in both C++ files, unify the ESS and Geweke $z$-score functions across Scripts 04 and 06, run at least 4 independent MCMC chains with longer post-burn-in retention (for example, 2,000 burn-in + 2,500 retained draws per chain) so that every parameter achieves multi-chain split-$\hat{R} < 1.02$, $\text{ESS} > 400$, and $|z_{\text{Geweke}}| < 2.0$, and add `lbcf_3wave` convergence diagnostics to Appendix Table 9.

5. **Prior specification on $\tau(W)$ and $\Sigma$, asymmetric $Z \in \{0, 1\}$ treatment coding, and confounded hyperparameters in Robustness Check `R6`.**  
   In `mvbcf_helpers.R` (lines 46 - 49) and `mvbcf_source_code.cpp` (lines 689 - 692), each outcome is standardised to unit variance (`y_scaled`), and the leaf priors are set to $\Sigma_\mu = \frac{1}{M_\mu} I_4$ and $\Sigma_\tau = \frac{1}{M_\tau} I_4$, with residual covariance prior $\Sigma_{\text{scaled}} \sim \mathcal{IW}(\nu_0 = 6, \Psi_0 = I_4)$. This raises three methodological issues in Bayesian causal forest design:
   - Because $Z_{i,t} \in \{0, 1\}$ is un-centred (rather than using the Hahn et al. 2020 treatment coding $b_{Z_i}$ with $b_0, b_1 \sim \mathcal{N}(0, 1/2)$), the prior predictive variance of $Y_{\text{scaled}}$ for treated units ($Z_{i,t} = 1$) is $\text{Var}(\mu) + \text{Var}(\tau) + \mathbb{E}[\Sigma] = 1 + 1 + 1 = 3$, whereas for control units ($Z_{i,t} = 0$) it is $1 + 0 + 1 = 2$, inducing artificial prior heteroskedasticity between treated and control units.
   - Setting the prior variance of the sum of $\tau$-trees equal to $1.0 = 100\%$ of the total observed outcome variance $\text{Var}(\Delta Y_j)$ is actually a *diffuse* scale prior on $\tau(W)$ (implying a prior SD of $\pm 2.57\text{ pp}$ on Non-Resource Tax / GDP and $\pm 5.23\text{ pp}$ on Direct Income Tax Share, which is why the conservative tree structure $\alpha_\tau = 0.25, \beta_\tau = 3.0$ is doing all the regularisation). Moreover, under $\mathcal{IW}(\nu_0 = 6, I_4)$ in dimension $D = 4$, the prior variance of $\Sigma_{jj}$ is infinite (requiring $\nu_0 > D + 3 = 7$ for finite variance).
   - In Robustness Check `R6` (`04_run_mvbcf_tax_models.R` line 681), the code simultaneously alters four hyperparameters (`n_tree_tau = 50`, `alpha_tau = 0.50`, `beta_tau = 2.0`, and `v_0 = 8`), while the text (line 196 and Table 7 line 174) describes `R6` as only changing $\alpha_\tau = 0.50, M_\tau = 50$ and never tests sensitivity to the leaf scale prior $\sigma_\tau^2$ (for example, $\sigma_\tau \in \{0.25, 0.50, 1.0\}$).  
   *Actionable fix:* Separate the prior sensitivity checks so that (i) tree structure ($\alpha_\tau, \beta_\tau, M_\tau$), (ii) leaf prior scale ($\sigma_\tau \in \{0.25, 0.50, 1.0\}$ or Hahn et al.'s $(b_0, b_1)$ half-Cauchy hyperprior), and (iii) Inverse-Wishart degrees of freedom ($\nu_0 \in \{6, 10, 15\}$ and diagonal scale $\Psi_0$) are varied one at a time in Appendix B.

6. **Flawed and incomplete baseline model comparisons (`B1` sample mismatch, `B3` propensity omission, `B4` omitting country fixed effects, and missing `GRF`, `Lasso`, and `RF` benchmarks).**  
   Section 5.4 and Table 6 (`tab:benchmarks`) contain several apples-to-oranges comparisons and omit key modern baselines:
   - **Sample size mismatch in `B1` (Univariate BCFs):** Section 5.4 (line 78) and the Table 6 caption (line 82) state that all benchmarks in Table 6 are *"fitted on the exact same dataset ($N = 283$, 112 countries)"*. However, in `04_run_mvbcf_tax_models.R` (line 478), `fit_mvbcf_spec` filters `complete.cases` on each outcome column individually, so `B1` is actually fitted on $N = 287, 284, 285, \text{ and } 285$ observations across $114, 112, 113, \text{ and } 114$ countries (`outputs/tables/table_method_benchmarks.csv` lines 6 - 9)! Thus, the comparison between joint `mvbcf` ($D=4$) and univariate BCF ($D=1$) is confounded by changing sample composition.
   - **Confounded ablation in `B3` (Single-Forest BART):** In `04_run_mvbcf_tax_models.R` (lines 489 - 515), `fit_bart_10y` omits the propensity score `pi_hat` from `X_con` while also collapsing $(\mu, \tau)$ into a single forest, thereby conflating the removal of separate $(\mu, \tau)$ regularisation with the removal of $\hat{\pi}(X_{i,t})$.
   - **Omission of Country Fixed Effects in `B4` and missing ML baselines:** Benchmark `B4` (`fit_lp_10y`, lines 538 - 550) is labelled `B4_Linear_LP_TWFE_10y` in the code, yet it includes only wave fixed effects (`wave_fe`) and regional/income-group controls, omitting **country fixed effects** (so it is pooled OLS with wave dummies, not Two-Way Fixed Effects). Meanwhile, in the actual within-country demeaned robustness check `R3b` at $+10$ years (`table4_robustness_checks.csv` lines 14 - 17), the Direct Income Tax / GDP effect drops by 45% to $+0.152\text{ pp}$ ($P = 0.814$) and Direct Income Tax Share drops by 52% to $+0.795\text{ pp}$ ($P = 0.874$), yet `R3b` is omitted from the rows of Table 7 in the manuscript (which only displays the $+5$-year `R3a` row)! Furthermore, although Section 2.3 (line 114) cites Athey, Tibshirani, and Wager (2019) on Generalised Random Forests (`GRF`), Table 6 includes no frequentist causal machine-learning baselines (`grf::causal_forest`, Double-ML `Lasso`, or Double-ML `Random Forests`) and no true Two-Way Fixed Effects (`TWFE`) estimator.  
   *Actionable fix:* Filter the dataset to the exact $N = 283$ four-outcome complete-case sample *before* running `B1`; include `pi_hat` in `B3` so the single-forest BART ablation isolates $(\mu, \tau)$ regularisation; expand Table 6 to report (i) Pooled OLS (`Linear LP` with Wave FE), (ii) true Two-Way Fixed Effects (`TWFE` with Country + Wave FE, demeaning both outcomes and regressors), (iii) Double-ML with `Lasso` (`glmnet`), (iv) Double-ML with `Random Forests` (`ranger`), and (v) frequentist Causal Forests (`grf::causal_forest`); and display the full $+10$-year country-demeaned row (`R3b`) in Table 7.

7. **Undisclosed ad-hoc formula for Non-Resource Tax Revenue, full-sample look-ahead in income-group treatment medians, and single-draw `R7` measurement-error check.**  
   Inspecting `src/r/02_build_clean_tax_panel.R` reveals three discrepancies between the manuscript's description of the data and what the code actually computes:
   - **Ad-hoc resource-rent subtraction formula (lines 200 - 207):** Section 3.2 (line 130) states that Outcome 1 ($\Delta \text{Tax/GDP}$) is taken from the UNU-WIDER Government Revenue Dataset adjusted to remove natural resource rents. In reality, `02_build_clean_tax_panel.R` (lines 200 - 204) constructs `non_resource_tax_gdp_pct` by applying an undisclosed piecewise linear heuristic to World Bank WDI tax revenue: $\max(\text{Tax/GDP} - 0.5 \max(\text{ResourceRents} - 10, 0), 2.0)$ (or $\max(\text{GovRev} - 0.7 \times \text{ResourceRents}, 2.0)$ when `tax_rev_gdp_pct` is missing), and then defines Outcome 2 (`inc_tax_gdp_pct`) by multiplying that heuristic quantity by `income_profit_tax_share_rev_pct / 100`. Notice that in Robustness Check `R1` (Table 7, Row 1), when pure unadjusted WDI Tax / GDP is used instead of this heuristic formula, the $+10$-year effect on Outcome 1 drops almost in half from $+0.53\text{ pp}$ ($P = 0.93$) to $+0.29\text{ pp}$ ($P = 0.77$).
   - **Full-panel look-ahead in treatment thresholds (lines 228 - 261):** The primary binary treatment `Z_regime_inc` compares a country's UNESCO spending ratio in wave $t$ (e.g. `1985-1989`) against `med_spend_raw`, which is computed by pooling all eight waves from `1985-1989` through `2020-2024`. Because per-student tertiary spending as a share of GDP per person declined secularly over 1985 - 2024 as enrolment expanded, computing the median over all 40 years uses future information to classify early waves and induces a secular downward trend in treatment assignment probability over time.
   - **Single-realisation perturbation in `R7` (lines 685 - 692 of `04_run_mvbcf_tax_models.R`):** Section 5.5 (line 196) states that `R7` propagates Monte Carlo measurement error ($\text{SD} = 1.5$ Gini points) across SWIID inequality estimates. In the code, `R7` draws a *single* random normal vector `rnorm(n(), 0, gini_se_clean)` (with missing SEs set to `1.0`, not `1.5`) and fits `mvbcf` once on that single noisy dataset rather than combining posterior draws across $M \ge 10$ independent measurement-error imputations (Rubin's rules / posterior draw pooling).  
   *Actionable fix:* Disclose the exact mathematical formula for `non_resource_tax_gdp_pct` and `inc_tax_gdp_pct` in Section 3.2 (and verify against the actual UNU-WIDER GRD non-resource tax series); add a robustness check defining $Z_{i,t}$ relative to wave-specific ($t$) or expanding pre-$t$ income-group medians; and in `R7`, pool posterior draws across at least $M = 10$ independent SWIID measurement-error realisations.

### 3. Minor concerns

1. **Phantom baseline covariates listed in Section 4.1 (`paper.tex` line 188):**  
   Section 4.1 (line 188) states that the prognostic baseline vector $X_{i,t}$ includes *"inflation"*, *"external debt"*, and *"institutional democracy"*. However, checking `base_con_vars` in `src/r/04_run_mvbcf_tax_models.R` (lines 94 - 114) and `src/r/02_build_clean_tax_panel.R` (lines 126 - 158) shows that none of these three variables exists in the processed dataset or enters the model (the 19 covariates in `base_con_vars` are `log_gdp_pc_ppp`, `non_resource_tax_gdp_pct`, `income_profit_tax_share_rev_pct`, `goods_services_tax_share_rev_pct`, `gini_disp_harmonised`, `life_expectancy_birth_yrs`, `gross_tert_enrolment_pct`, `gross_seco_enrolment_pct`, `edu_spend_total_gdp_pct`, `working_age_pop_15_64_pct`, `log_population`, `unemployment_rate_pct`, `natural_resource_rents_gdp_pct`, `trade_openness_gdp_pct`, `lag_gdp_pc_growth_pct`, `income_group_num`, `region_num`, `is_oecd`, and `wave_index`). Correct line 188 so the text matches the exact 19 variables in the code.

2. **Linear addition of quantile half-width and $1.96\bar{\sigma}$ in Posterior Predictive Check (PPC) 95% coverage (`04_run_mvbcf_tax_models.R` lines 768 - 769):**  
   In `compute_ppc_metrics`, the 95% posterior predictive interval for unit $i$ is computed as `quantile(pred_mat, 0.025) - 1.96 * mean(sig_draws)` to `quantile(pred_mat, 0.975) + 1.96 * mean(sig_draws)`. Adding standard deviations linearly instead of adding variances in quadrature (by drawing $y^{\text{rep},(s)}_i \sim \mathcal{N}(\mu^{(s)}_i + \tau^{(s)}_i Z_i, (\sigma^{(s)}_j)^2)$ at each MCMC iteration $s$ and taking the 2.5% and 97.5% quantiles of $y^{\text{rep},(s)}_i$) artificially widens every predictive interval. This explains why `Cov95` in Appendix Table 9 (`tab:mcmc`, lines 293 - 307) systematically over-covers at $97.6\%$ to $99.3\%$ across every single outcome. Replace lines 768 - 769 with proper posterior predictive draws $y^{\text{rep},(s)}_i$.

3. **Moderator importance (`Woody et al. 2021`) computed only on point estimates and partial-$R^2$ share interpretation (`04_run_mvbcf_tax_models.R` lines 614 - 640; `paper.tex` line 129):**  
   Section 5.4 (line 129) states that *"we also project the unit-level CATE posterior draws onto the moderator matrix $W_{i,t}$"* and that four moderators *"account for the vast majority of systematic CATE variation (surrogate $R^2 = 45.3\%$): OECD membership (8.0%...), working-age population share (5.9%...), five-year wave time index (4.6%), and baseline log real GDP per person (2.3%...)"*. In `04_run_mvbcf_tax_models.R` (line 615), the linear projection is fitted only to the posterior mean vector `tau_hat_j <- rowMeans(...)` rather than projecting each MCMC draw $s$ to obtain posterior credible intervals on the surrogate coefficients and $R^2$ as in Woody et al. (2021).
