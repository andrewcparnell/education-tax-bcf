# Phase 6 Mechanisms, Accounting Identities, and Plausibility Review (Reviewer 3)

**Manuscript:** *Does Increasing Government Spending on Tertiary Education Improve the Future Tax Take? A Bayesian Causal Inference Analysis* (`paper/paper.tex` and `paper/paper.md`)  
**Reviewer Role:** Reviewer 3 (Mechanisms, Accounting Identities, and Macroeconomic Plausibility Expert)  
**Recommendation:** **Minor Revision**

---

### 1. Overall Impression

The continuous-treatment redesign of this manuscript is a substantial methodological and substantive leap forward. Replacing the binary regime indicator with a continuous university funding dose ($Z_{i,t} \in [0, 3.0]$ in Scaling A per $+10\text{ pp}$ of GDP per person per student, and $Z^{\text{log}}_{i,t} \in [0, 3.0]$ in Scaling B per $+25\%$ log-relative expansion), paired with country-blocked out-of-bag Random Forest conditioning $\hat{z}(X_{i,t}) = \mathbb{E}[Z_{i,t} \mid X_{i,t}]$ and a locked 20% unseen-country holdout test set (22 countries, 54 observations at $+10$ years), resolves the core plausibility and threshold-cliff problems of the earlier binary specification. Under the continuous $+10\text{ pp}$ dose (`M2b`), the estimated 10-year direct slopes - $+0.71\text{ pp}$ on the direct income tax share of revenue ($P = 0.96$), $+0.12\text{ pp}$ on direct income tax as a percentage of GDP ($P = 0.90$), and $+0.24\text{ pp}$ on total non-resource tax as a percentage of GDP ($P = 0.89$) - are macroeconomically realistic, consistent with canonical human-capital and formal-sector earnings elasticities, and immune to the artificial step-function cliffs that previously assigned identical full-treatment jumps to near-boundary countries such as New Zealand ($-0.31\text{ pp}$ below the high-income median) and Iceland ($-0.35\text{ pp}$ below the median). Moreover, the relationship between the 10-year Total Fiscal Effect (`M1b`: $+0.30\text{ pp}$ on total tax/GDP and $+0.16\text{ pp}$ on direct income tax/GDP) and the Mediator-Removed Direct Effect (`M2b`: $+0.24\text{ pp}$ and $+0.12\text{ pp}$) is now theoretically coherent: roughly one-fifth to one-quarter of the 10-year aggregate tax-to-GDP gain operates through overall output expansion ($\Delta \log \text{GDP}_{t+1}$ and $\Delta \text{Enrol}_{t+1}$), whilst the structural shift in the tax mix toward direct income taxation ($+0.54\text{ pp}$ in `M1b` versus $+0.71\text{ pp}$ in `M2b`) is a direct human-capital formalisation effect.

At the same time, my audit of the data-construction pipeline (`src/r/02_build_clean_tax_panel.R`), the C++ sampler (`src/cpp/mvbcf_source_code.cpp`), the diagnostic scripts (`src/r/06_generate_figures_and_diagnostics.R`), and the manuscript text (`paper/paper.tex` and `paper/paper.md`) uncovered three substantive mechanism and accounting issues that should be addressed before publication. First, in low-income countries, pre-winsorising `tert_spend_per_student_winsor` at $150\%$ *before* centring and winsorising `Z_cont_10pp` at $\pm 1.5$ dose units collapses the upper half of the low-income distribution ($\ge 150\%$, where the raw training median is $183.8\%$) to a single mass point at $Z_{i,t} = 1.50$, and creates a mismatch between the group medians reported in `paper/paper.tex` (`med_spend_raw`: $183.8\%, 68.9\%, 27.6\%, 26.4\%$) and those used in `02_build_clean_tax_panel.R` (`med_spend_winsor`: $150.0\%, 71.1\%, 27.9\%, 26.4\%$). Second, while the bilinear tax-accounting identity ($Y^{(2)} = Y^{(1)} \times Y^{(3)} / 100$) holds closely for the baseline level models (`M1b` and `M2b`), the within-country 5-year expansion model (`M3b`, Table 3 Row 2) estimates $\tau_2 = +0.37\text{ pp}$ on direct income tax/GDP alongside $\tau_1 = -0.29\text{ pp}$ on total tax/GDP and $\tau_3 = +1.56\text{ pp}$ on direct income tax share, which benefits from explicit compositional reconciliation in the text. Third, the manuscript attributes the $-0.06\text{ pp}$ dip in total tax/GDP at the $+5$-year horizon (`M2a`) to a temporary "student lock-in" effect (young adults remaining in lecture theatres rather than working), yet `M2a` conditions on forward tertiary enrolment expansion ($\Delta \text{Enrol}_{t+1}$) and produces virtually the same $+5$-year slope as `M1a` ($-0.05\text{ pp}$). Addressing these three points, alongside minor synchronisation fixes between `paper.tex` and `paper.md`, will make the paper rock-solid.

### 2. Major Concerns

#### 1. Pre-Winsorisation of $Z_{i,t}$ in Low-Income Countries (`150%` Cap Before Centring) and Discrepancy Between `med_spend_raw` and `med_spend_winsor`
- **Mechanism and Accounting Issue:** In `src/r/02_build_clean_tax_panel.R` (lines 226-227 and 274-306), the raw UNESCO tertiary spending intensity (`tert_spend_raw`) is first winsorised at $150.0\%$ of GDP per person (`tert_spend_per_student_winsor = pmin(pmax(tert_spend_raw, 1.0), 150.0)`), and then `Z_cont_10pp` (Scaling A) is constructed by centring `tert_spend_per_student_winsor` on its training-set group median (`med_spend_winsor`) and clipping at $\pm 1.5$ dose units ($\pm 15\text{ pp}$).
  In `paper/paper.tex` (Section 3.3, line 167, and Table 8 caption, line 617), the text states that $Z_{i,t}$ is centred on the raw training-set medians (`183.8%` for low income, `68.9%` for lower-middle, `27.6%` for upper-middle, and `26.4%` for high income), whereas `paper/paper.md` (Section 3.3, line 98) and `02_build_clean_tax_panel.R` (line 300) centre on the pre-winsorised training medians (`150.0%`, `71.1%`, `27.9%`, and `26.4%`). Furthermore, in `06_generate_figures_and_diagnostics.R` (lines 1181-1196), Table 8 (`tableA4_latest_z0_countries_costs_and_returns.csv`) filters below-median countries using `Z_cont_10pp < 1.5` (which uses `med_spend_winsor`), but computes `gap_to_median_pp = med_spend_raw - tert_spend_raw` (which uses `med_spend_raw`).
- **Actionable Fix:** Document clearly in `paper.tex` Section 3.3 that Scaling A caps raw spending at $150\%$ prior to centring (reporting both the pre-winsorised training medians $150.0\%, 71.1\%, 27.9\%, 26.4\%$ used in Scaling A and the raw training medians $183.8\%, 68.9\%, 27.6\%, 26.4\%$ used in Scaling B), note that low-income variation above $150\%$ is captured by Scaling B (`Z_cont_log25pct`) and the unwinsorised specification (`R1b`), and use `med_spend_winsor` consistently in `06_generate_figures_and_diagnostics.R` when computing `gap_to_median_pp` for Scaling A in Table 8 (`tableA4`).

#### 2. Accounting Reconciliation of the Bilinear Identity ($Y^{(2)} = Y^{(1)} \times Y^{(3)} / 100$) Between Baseline Levels (`M2b`) and Within-Country Expansions (`M3b`)
- **Mechanism and Accounting Issue:** As noted in Section 3.2, the first three outcomes are linked by the exact accounting identity:
  $$\text{Direct Income Tax / GDP } (Y^{(2)}_{i,t}) = \frac{\text{Total Non-Resource Tax / GDP } (Y^{(1)}_{i,t}) \times \text{Direct Income Tax Share } (Y^{(3)}_{i,t})}{100}.$$
  Taking a first-order Taylor approximation of the 10-year change around the sample means ($\bar{Y}^{(1)} \approx 17.0\%$ of GDP and $\bar{Y}^{(3)} \approx 34.4\%$ of revenue) yields the linearised accounting relation:
  $$\tau_2 \approx \frac{\bar{Y}^{(3)}}{100}\,\tau_1 + \frac{\bar{Y}^{(1)}}{100}\,\tau_3 = 0.344\,\tau_1 + 0.170\,\tau_3.$$
  In the primary level models (`M1b` and `M2b`), the MV-BCF posterior means satisfy this accounting identity closely without imposing it as a hard constraint. However, in the **Within-Country 5y Change model (`M3b`, Table 3 Row 2)**, the estimated slopes per $+10\text{ pp}$ within-country expansion are $\tau_1 = -0.29\text{ pp}$ ($P = 0.17$) on total non-resource tax/GDP, $\tau_3 = +1.56\text{ pp}$ ($P = 0.99$) on the direct income tax share, and $\tau_2 = +0.37\text{ pp}$ ($P = 1.00$) on direct income tax/GDP. Evaluated at unweighted sample means, $\tau_1 = -0.29$ and $\tau_3 = +1.56$ would imply $\tau_2 \approx +0.16\text{ pp}$, which is less than half of the estimated $+0.37\text{ pp}$.
- **Actionable Fix:** In Section 5.2 (`paper.tex` line 287), add a brief explanation noting that because each outcome is modelled via its own flexible tree ensemble in MV-BCF and countries with higher baseline tax-to-GDP ratios ($Y^{(1)}_{i,t}$ of $25\text{--}35\%$ of GDP) experience larger compositional shifts when expanding per-student funding, the directly estimated $\tau_2 = +0.37\text{ pp}$ exceeds the unweighted sample-mean product approximation whilst total tax-to-GDP dips slightly as governments phase out indirect trade and excise taxes ($-0.94\text{ pp}$ on the goods tax share).

#### 3. Reconciling the $+5$-Year "Student Lock-In" Explanation with Conditioning on Forward Enrolment ($\Delta \text{Enrol}_{t+1}$)
- **Mechanism and Plausibility Issue:** Across Section 5.1, Section 5.3, and Section 6.1, the manuscript explains the slightly negative $+5$-year slope on total non-resource tax/GDP (`M2a`: $-0.06\text{ pp}$, $P = 0.29$; and $-0.30\text{ pp}$ in low-income countries) as a temporary "student lock-in" effect, arguing that better-funded universities retain more young adults in lecture theatres during the first 5-year window rather than sending them immediately into taxable employment. Because `M2a` already conditions on the $+5$-year change in gross tertiary enrolment (`d_t1_tert_enrol_pp`) inside the prognostic forest $\mu(\cdot)$, extensive-margin headcount enrolment expansion is held constant in `M2a`.
- **Actionable Fix:** Refine the mechanism discussion in Sections 5.3 and 6.1 to clarify that even holding aggregate headcount enrolment growth fixed, higher per-student funding increases **intensive-margin study duration and full-time persistence** (reducing student drop-out and part-time casual work while students complete 3-to-4-year degrees and postgraduate training), so cohorts remain in full-time study during years 0 to 4 before entering formal tax-paying careers over the 10-year horizon.

### 3. Evaluation of Plausibility, Competing Explanations, and Novelty (plus Minor Concerns)

1. **Macroeconomic Plausibility of the Continuous Effect Sizes:** Against a median $\approx 0.40\text{ pp}$ of GDP upfront investment for a $+10\text{ pp}$ dose (`0.28 to 0.32 pp` in high-income countries), `M2b` estimates a $+10$-year direct return of $+0.12\text{ pp}$ of GDP in direct income and profit taxes (and $+0.24\text{ pp}$ of GDP in total non-resource taxes; $+0.30\text{ pp}$ in total effect `M1b`). Over a 30-to-40-year working career, the cumulative discounted fiscal return comfortably exceeds the upfront cost without requiring implausibly large Mincer returns.
2. **Asymmetry of Prior Predictive Variance from Shifting $Z_{i,t} \in [-1.5, +1.5]$ to $[0, 3.0]$:** Adding one phrase in Appendix A noting why $Z_{i,t}$ is shifted to $[0, 3.0]$ (so that $Z_{i,t} \ge 0$ preserves non-negative dose support in the C++ BCF sampler while $\mu(X_{i,t}, \hat{z}_{i,t})$ absorbs the baseline intercept at $Z_{i,t} = 0$) would be helpful for technical readers.
3. **Evaluation of Competing Theoretical Explanations and Literature Novelty:** The pre-treatment placebo test (`R5`), the within-country demeaned fixed-effects model (`R3b`), the within-country 5-year change dose (`M3b`), and the opposite signs on Outcomes 3 and 4 convincingly address reverse causality and concurrent VAT reforms.
4. **Synchronisation Discrepancies Between `paper/paper.md` and `paper/paper.tex` (`outputs/tables/`):** Synchronise the remaining minor text numbers in `paper/paper.md` (lines 98, 128-131, 137, 217, and 231) with `paper/paper.tex` and `outputs/tables/`.
