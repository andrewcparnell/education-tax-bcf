# Referee Report: Reviewer 4 (Independent Blind External Academic Referee)

**Manuscript Title:** *Does Increasing Government Spend on Tertiary Education Improve the Future Tax Take? A Bayesian Causal Inference Analysis*  
**Files Reviewed:** `paper/paper.tex`, `paper/paper.md`, `paper/references.bib`, `outputs/figures/*.png`, `outputs/tables/*.csv`, and supporting R/C++ source code (`src/r/`, `src/cpp/`).

---

### 1. Overall impression

This manuscript investigates an important macro-fiscal question: whether public investment per tertiary student increases a country's future tax capacity and shifts revenue structure toward direct income, profit, and capital gains taxation over 5-year and 10-year horizons, net of general macroeconomic growth. The conceptual framing in Sections 1 to 3 is clear and well motivated. In particular, the distinction between aggregate tertiary budget shares and per-student resource intensity (the "UNESCO denominator trap"), the joint modelling of four pure percentage-point fiscal outcomes without mechanical GDP product identities, the use of indirect goods and services taxation as a negative-control channel, and the combination of Multivariate Bayesian Causal Forests (`mvbcf`) with Three-Wave Longitudinal Bayesian Causal Forests (`lbcf`) represent a thoughtful empirical design. The repository includes raw data checksums, C++ (`RcppArmadillo`) and R scripts, and compiled tables and figures.

Despite these strengths, a rigorous audit of the manuscript against the underlying data pipeline, C++ samplers, CSV outputs, figures, and bibliography reveals several severe methodological, data-provenance, and internal-consistency flaws that preclude publication in its current form. Most critically: (1) the custom C++ BART/BCF tree samplers (`mvbcf_source_code.cpp` and `lbcf_3wave.cpp`) omit the Metropolis-Hastings proposal transition ratio for Grow and Prune steps, violating detailed balance and sampling from an incorrect tree posterior; (2) the three-wave longitudinal script (`05_run_longitudinal_bcf_tax.R`) feeds 10-year future macroeconomic changes (`d_t2_*`) into the 5-year Wave 1 growth forest $\mu_1$ (constituting future-information leakage) and assumes i.i.d. errors across stacked country-waves without country random intercepts; (3) the "Mediator-Removed Direct Fiscal Effect" (`M2`) conditions directly on contemporaneous 10-year post-treatment outcomes/mediators (such as 10-year GDP growth) inside $\mu(\cdot)$, inducing post-treatment collider bias rather than implementing sequential g-estimation or Bayesian causal mediation forests; (4) the text claims to use the UNU-WIDER Government Revenue Dataset (GRD) to construct non-resource tax revenue, whereas the actual code never loads UNU-WIDER GRD and instead applies an undisclosed heuristic formula to World Bank WDI data; and (5) there are numerous signs of unchecked AI-assisted drafting and selective reporting, including fabricated covariate descriptions in the text ("inflation", "external debt", "institutional democracy") that do not exist in the code, omission of the 10-year country fixed-effects robustness row (`R3b`, where the effects become statistically indistinguishable from zero) from Table 7, two conflicting sets of MCMC diagnostics across the pipeline scripts, and an invalid formula for posterior predictive coverage. My recommendation is **Reject and Resubmit (or Major Revision with complete re-estimation)**.

### 2. Major concerns

1. **Invalid Metropolis-Hastings acceptance ratio in the C++ `mvbcf` and `lbcf` tree samplers (`src/cpp/mvbcf_source_code.cpp` and `src/cpp/lbcf_3wave.cpp`).**  
   In both `mvbcf_source_code.cpp` (lines 771 - 788 and 840 - 859) and `lbcf_3wave.cpp` (lines 716 - 733, 785 - 804, 857 - 875, 928 - 946, and 1000 - 1018), the Metropolis-Hastings acceptance probability for tree structure updates (`Grow`, `Prune`, `Change`, `Swap`) is coded simply as `double a = exp(lnew - lold);`, where `log_lik()` and `log_lik_tau()` return the sum of the integrated log marginal likelihood $\log P(Y \mid X, T)$ and the log tree prior $\log P(T)$. As established by Chipman, George, and McCulloch (1998, 2010), the reversible-jump / Metropolis-Hastings acceptance ratio for `Grow` and `Prune` moves must include the proposal transition ratio $q(T^* \to T) / q(T \to T^*)$ (which depends on the number of terminal leaves $|\mathcal{T}_{\text{leaves}}|$, the number of singly internal parent nodes $|\mathcal{T}_{\text{nogs}}|$, and the number of available split variables and split values at the chosen node). Omitting $q(T^* \to T) / q(T \to T^*)$ violates detailed balance and systematically biases the depth and topology of the sampled trees. In addition, in `grow()` (`mvbcf_source_code.cpp` lines 305 - 329), if a proposed split violates `min_nodesize`, the function exits silently without modifying `proposal_tree`, which then gets evaluated with `lnew == lold` (`a = 1.0`) as a vacuous move.  
   *Concrete fix:* Multiply `exp(lnew - lold)` by the exact Chipman et al. (2010) Hastings proposal ratio for `Grow` and `Prune` moves, reject invalid split proposals immediately before computing `log_lik`, and validate the corrected C++ sampler against an established package (such as `bcf` or `dbarts`) on synthetic benchmarks.

2. **Future-information leakage (lookahead bias) and violated i.i.d. error assumptions in the Three-Wave Longitudinal BCF (`src/r/05_run_longitudinal_bcf_tax.R`).**  
   Inspecting `05_run_longitudinal_bcf_tax.R` (lines 94 - 98 and 120 - 124) reveals two serious flaws in the longitudinal specification (`Table 5` and `Figure 5`). First, `growth_vars_med` stacks both 5-year mediators (`d_t1_log_gdp_pc_pct`, `d_t1_tert_enrol_pp`) and 10-year mediators (`d_t2_log_gdp_pc_pct`, `d_t2_tert_enrol_pp`) into `X_growth_stack`, which is passed as the predictor matrix for **both** the Wave 1 growth forest $\mu_1$ ($t \to t+1$, years 0 to 5) and the Wave 2 growth forest $\mu_2$ ($t+1 \to t+2$, years 5 to 10). Consequently, the 5-year prognostic forest $\mu_1$ splits on macroeconomic and enrolment changes that occur between years 5 and 10 (`d_t2_*`), violating temporal causality. Moreover, `growth_vars_med` includes only two of the four mediators used in `M2`, omitting `gini_disp` and `life_exp_yrs` without explanation. Second, Equation (4) (`paper.tex` line 247, appendix) stacks the three outcome levels $(Y_{i,0}, Y_{i,1}, Y_{i,2})$ for each country-wave trajectory into a $3N = 846$ vector with a single homoscedastic i.i.d. Gaussian error $\epsilon_{i,w} \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \tau^{-1})$ (`lbcf_3wave.cpp` line 1066) and removes the baseline outcome level $Y_{i,0}$ from $\mu_0(X_{i,t})$. Because cross-country tax levels vary from under 10% to over 40% of GDP, any unexplained baseline country level $Y_{i,0} - \mu_0(X_{i,t})$ persists across all three waves $w \in \{0, 1, 2\}$, inducing severe within-trajectory error correlation and inflating the residual variance. This misspecification also explains why `lbcf` (Table 5) produces a negative 5-year effect (`-0.06` pp, $P = 0.36$) and weak 10-year effect (`+0.14` pp, $P = 0.79$) on Direct Income Tax / GDP, contradicting the `mvbcf` estimates (`+0.20` pp and `+0.28` pp) in Table 2.  
   *Concrete fix:* Restrict $\mu_1$ to covariates and mediators realised at or before $t+1$, include all four mediators consistently, incorporate trajectory-level random intercepts (or model wave-to-wave differences $\Delta Y_{i, t\to t+1}$ and $\Delta Y_{i, t+1\to t+2}$ jointly), and explicitly discuss the discrepancy between Table 2 and Table 5 on Outcome 2.

3. **Post-treatment collider bias in the "Mediator-Removed Direct Fiscal Effect" (`M2`) and full-panel lookahead in treatment construction.**  
   To isolate the direct fiscal effect (`M2`), `04_run_mvbcf_tax_models.R` (lines 129 - 130, 197, 320 - 321) simply appends four contemporaneous 5-year or 10-year change variables (`d_t2_log_gdp_pc_pct`, `d_t2_tert_enrol_pp`, `d_t2_gini_disp`, `d_t2_life_exp_yrs`) to the baseline covariate matrix $X_{i,t}$ inside the prognostic forest $\mu(\cdot)$. While the manuscript cites Acharya, Blackwell, and Sen (2016), Imai et al. (2010), and VanderWeele (2016) to justify this adjustment, Acharya et al. (2016) specifically prove that conditioning directly on post-treatment mediators in a single-stage outcome regression induces **post-treatment collider / intermediate-variable bias** whenever unobserved post-treatment shocks (such as commodity booms, fiscal consolidations, or macroeconomic crises over $[t, t+2]$) jointly affect both 10-year GDP growth and 10-year tax revenue changes. That is why Table 2 shows that the "mediator-removed direct effect" (`M2`) is actually *larger* than the total effect (`M1`) across multiple outcomes (`+0.53` vs `+0.37` pp on Non-Resource Tax / GDP; `+1.66` vs `+1.38` pp on Direct Income Tax Share), which implies a negative indirect effect through GDP growth and enrolment expansion that is never diagnosed or explained. Furthermore, in `02_build_clean_tax_panel.R` (lines 228 - 236 and 260 - 283), the binary treatment indicators (`Z_regime_inc`, `Z_real_spend_regime`, `Z_tert_gdp_regime`) are defined relative to income-group medians computed across **all 8 waves pooled together (1985 - 2024)**, meaning that treatment status in 1985 is defined using future spending distributions up to 2024.  
   *Concrete fix:* Implement proper two-stage sequential g-estimation (demediation, as in Acharya et al., 2016) or Bayesian causal mediation tree ensembles (Linero and Zhang, 2025; Ting and Linero, 2025), add a formal sensitivity analysis for post-treatment mediator-outcome confounding, and compute income-group treatment thresholds strictly within each wave $t$ (or expanding pre-$t$ windows).

4. **Undisclosed synthetic construction of the primary outcome vs claimed use of the UNU-WIDER Government Revenue Dataset.**  
   In Section 3.1 (`paper.tex` line 121), Section 3.2 (line 130), and the Data Availability Statement (line 226), the manuscript states that the panel merges the UNU-WIDER Government Revenue Dataset (`prichard2016reassessing`, `ictd2023grdv`) to measure domestic non-resource tax revenue. However, inspecting `data/raw/PROVENANCE.md`, `src/r/01_download_tax_education_data.R`, and `src/r/02_build_clean_tax_panel.R` (lines 200 - 208) reveals that **no UNU-WIDER GRD file is ever downloaded or read**. Instead, `non_resource_tax_gdp_pct` is fabricated from World Bank WDI `tax_rev_gdp_pct` (`GC.TAX.TOTL.GD.ZS`) and `gov_rev_ex_grants_gdp_pct` (`GC.REV.XGRT.GD.ZS`) via an undisclosed piecewise formula using WDI natural resource rents (`NY.GDP.TOTL.RT.ZS`):
   $$\text{NonResTax}_{i,t} = \max\bigl(\text{Tax/GDP}_{i,t} - 0.5 \max(\text{Rents}_{i,t} - 10, 0),\; 2.0\bigr),$$
   or $\max(\text{GovRev/GDP}_{i,t} - 0.7\,\text{Rents}_{i,t},\; 2.0)$ when `tax_rev_gdp_pct` is missing. Then, Outcome 2 (`inc_tax_gdp_pct`) is constructed on line 206 by multiplying this synthetic `non_resource_tax_gdp_pct` by `income_profit_tax_share_rev_pct / 100` (`GC.TAX.YPKG.ZS`, whose denominator in WDI/IMF GFS is *total government revenue* including non-tax revenue, not non-resource tax revenue). Notice that when raw unadjusted WDI tax-to-GDP is used in Robustness Check `R1` (Table 7), the 10-year effect on Tax / GDP drops nearly in half from `+0.53` pp ($P = 0.93$) to `+0.29` pp ($P = 0.77$).  
   *Concrete fix:* Either merge the actual UNU-WIDER GRD series for non-resource tax revenue and direct income tax to GDP, or remove the false claim that UNU-WIDER GRD data were analysed, state Equation (lines 200 - 208 of `02_build_clean_tax_panel.R`) explicitly in Section 3.2, and justify the arbitrary `0.5`, `10%`, `0.7`, and `2.0%` floor parameters.

5. **Selective reporting of robustness checks (`R3b` omission), cherry-picked subgroups (Table 4), and switching between 95% and 80% credible intervals.**  
   There are three troubling patterns of selective reporting across the text and tables:
   - *Omission of `R3b` (+10y Country Fixed Effects) from Table 7:* Every row of Table 7 (`tab:robust`) evaluates the $+10$-year horizon except Row 3 (`R3a`), which abruptly switches to the $+5$-year horizon. Inspecting `outputs/tables/table4_robustness_checks.csv` (lines 14 - 17) shows that the script *did* estimate the $+10$-year within-country demeaned fixed-effects specification (`R3b_CountryFE_Demean_10y`), where all three primary effects fall below the paper's $P \ge 0.90$ threshold: Non-Resource Tax / GDP drops to `+0.41` pp ($P = 0.896$, 95% CrI `[-0.23, +1.07]`), Direct Income Tax / GDP drops by nearly half to `+0.15` pp ($P = 0.814$, 95% CrI `[-0.19, +0.48]`), and Direct Income Tax Share drops by more than half from `+1.66` pp to `+0.80` pp ($P = 0.875$, 95% CrI `[-0.61, +2.05]`). Relegating only the $+0.41$ pp number to a parenthetical remark in the text while omitting `R3b` from Table 7 conceals the fact that the headline 10-year direct income tax effects are not robust to country fixed effects.
   - *Selective switching between 95% CrIs and 80% CrIs in the Abstract and main text:* Whenever a 95% credible interval excludes zero (for example, Outcome 3 in `M2`: `[+0.08, +2.19]` and `[+0.13, +3.30]`, or `lbcf` Outcome 1: `[+0.04, +1.39]`), the Abstract and Section 5.1 report the **95% CrI**. Whenever the 95% CrI includes zero (for example, `M2` Outcome 1 at $+10\text{y}$ where the 95% CrI is `[-0.16, +1.23]`, or Outcome 2 at $+5\text{y}$ and $+10\text{y}$ where the 95% CrIs are `[-0.06, +0.46]` and `[-0.10, +0.68]`), the Abstract and text switch to reporting the **80% CrI** (`[+0.07, +1.00]`, `[+0.04, +0.37]`, and `[+0.02, +0.53]`), or omit the interval altogether (as for `lbcf` Outcome 3 in the Abstract, where the 95% CrI is `[-0.05, +3.01]`).
   - *Cherry-picked rows and undisclosed separate model in Table 4 (`tab:subgroups`):* Table 4 includes only 6 of the 15 rows from `outputs/tables/table3_subgroup_cates.csv`, omitting `Low income` ($N = 15, C = 9$) and `Lower middle income` ($N = 47, C = 21$) as well as 4 of the 5 OECD financing regimes, and fails to disclose that Row 6 (`OECD Nordic High-Public`) is extracted from a completely separate subsample model (`m4_oecd_10y`, fitted only on OECD countries with $M_\mu = 50, M_\tau = 25$) rather than `m2_10y`.  
   *Concrete fix:* Report 95% credible intervals uniformly throughout the Abstract, text, and all tables (including Tables 3 and 7, which currently omit credible intervals altogether); include `R3b` (`+10y Country Fixed Effects`) in Table 7 and discuss the attenuation honestly; and report all income groups and OECD regimes in Table 4 with clear attribution of `m4_oecd_10y`.

6. **Single-chain MCMC inference, conflicting diagnostic formulas across scripts, and an invalid Posterior Predictive Check (PPC) coverage formula.**  
   All Bayesian models are run as a **single MCMC chain** of 2,000 iterations (750 burn-in, 1,250 retained draws). Running a single short chain cannot detect multi-modal tree posteriors or between-chain non-convergence. Worse, the repository contains two contradictory sets of MCMC diagnostics for the exact same `M2` posterior draws:
   - In `04_run_mvbcf_tax_models.R` (`outputs/tables/table1_mvbcf_ate_posteriors.csv`, lines 10 - 17), `calc_geweke_z()` yields large Geweke $|z| > 2$ values on **5 of the 8 `M2` parameters** (`+2.98`, `-4.07`, `-3.88`, `-2.83`, `-2.44`) and up to `7.60` on `M1` (`+10y Goods Tax Share`) and `-8.30` on `M3f`, indicating severe non-stationarity in the single chain.
   - In `06_generate_figures_and_diagnostics.R` (lines 570 - 580, reported in Appendix Table A2 / `tab:mcmc`), a second function `compute_geweke_z()` replaces the sample sizes $n_1, n_2$ in the denominator with small autocorrelation-adjusted effective sample sizes `ess1, ess2` (floored at 10 on a window of 125 draws), which artificially inflates the denominator and shrinks every Geweke $z$-score in Table A2 below 2.0 (`+1.54`, `-1.94`, `-1.25`, `-1.01`, `-1.10`, `-0.13`). Even in Table A2, Row 8 (`+10y Goods Tax Share`) has an effective sample size of only **$\text{ESS} = 58$** (below the standard $\text{ESS} \ge 100$ minimum for tail quantiles), which is visually confirmed by the low-frequency drift in the orange trace of Panel 4 in Figure A1 (`figA1_mcmc_convergence_traces.png`).
   - Furthermore, the 95% Posterior Predictive Coverage (`Cov95` in Table A2) is computed in `04_run_mvbcf_tax_models.R` (lines 768 - 769) as:
     ```r
     q_low  <- apply(pred_mat, 1, quantile, probs = 0.025) - 1.96 * mean(sig_draws)
     q_high <- apply(pred_mat, 1, quantile, probs = 0.975) + 1.96 * mean(sig_draws)
     ```
     Adding the 95% interval half-width of the conditional mean $\mu_i + \tau_i Z_i$ linearly to $1.96 \bar{\sigma}$ sums standard deviations instead of variances ($\text{SD}_{\text{mean}} + \text{SD}_{\varepsilon} > \sqrt{\text{SD}_{\text{mean}}^2 + \text{SD}_{\varepsilon}^2}$), artificially inflating `Cov95` to 97.6% - 99.6%.  
   *Concrete fix:* Run at least 4 independent MCMC chains with longer burn-in (for example, 4 chains $\times$ 5,000 iterations) to compute genuine multi-chain split-$\hat{R}$ and bulk/tail ESS (Vehtari et al., 2021), reconcile the diagnostic functions across scripts, and compute posterior predictive intervals by drawing $y_i^{\text{rep},(s)} \sim \mathcal{N}_4(\mu_i^{(s)} + \tau_i^{(s)} Z_i, \Sigma^{(s)})$ at each MCMC draw $s$.

7. **Hallucinated/inaccurate descriptions of covariates and methods in the manuscript text vs actual code (`paper.tex` vs `src/r/`).**  
   Several specific methodological claims in the manuscript contradict the actual code, a hallmark of unchecked LLM drafting:
   - *Non-existent covariates claimed in Section 4.1 (`paper.tex` line 188):* The text states that $X_{i,t}$ controls for `"inflation"`, `"external debt"`, and `"institutional democracy"`. None of these three variables exist in `base_con_vars` (`04_run_mvbcf_tax_models.R` lines 94 - 114) or in `clean_tax_panel.csv` (which instead uses `life_expectancy_birth_yrs`, `edu_spend_total_gdp_pct`, `income_group_num`, `region_num`, `is_oecd`, and `wave_index`). Moreover, `region_num` encodes 7 unordered geographic regions as a single integer $1, \dots, 7$, imposing an arbitrary alphabetical ordering on tree splits.
   - *Impossible wave claim in Robustness Check `R2` (`paper.tex` line 193):* The text claims `R2` restricts the $+10$-year sample to four non-overlapping decadal waves: `1990-1994`, `2000-2004`, `2010-2014`, and `2020-2024` ($N = 154$). In fact, `04_run_mvbcf_tax_models.R` (line 655) filters `wave_index %in% c(2, 4, 6)` (only the first three waves, since `2020-2024` has no $+10$-year follow-up in 2030 - 2034).
   - *Incorrect description of Robustness Check `R7` (`paper.tex` line 196):* The text claims `R7` propagates `"Monte Carlo measurement error (SD = 1.5 Gini points)"`, whereas `04_run_mvbcf_tax_models.R` (lines 685 - 692) adds a *single* random normal draw using `swiid_gini_disp_se` (with `1.0`, not `1.5`, for missing values) and fits the model once, which is a single-draw perturbation rather than Monte Carlo error propagation.
   - *Conflated Benchmark `B1` and `B3` specifications (`paper.tex` lines 78 - 127):* Section 5.4 and Table 6 state that all benchmarks are fitted on the exact same dataset ($N = 283, C = 112$). However, `outputs/tables/table_method_benchmarks.csv` (lines 6 - 9) shows that `B1` (`Univariate BCFs`) was fitted on $N = 287, 284, 285, 285$ across $112\text{ - }114$ countries because `complete.cases()` was applied outcome by outcome. Meanwhile, `B3` (`Single-Forest BART`, lines 489 - 535 of `04_run_mvbcf_tax_models.R`) omits the propensity score $\hat{\pi}(X_{i,t})$ and uses 1,500 iterations (500 burn-in) instead of 2,000 iterations (750 burn-in), confounding the $(\mu, \tau)$ split ablation with propensity-score omission.  
   *Concrete fix:* Audit every paragraph of Sections 3, 4, and 5 against `02_build_clean_tax_panel.R` and `04_run_mvbcf_tax_models.R` so that every covariate, wave filter, sample size, and benchmark specification matches the code exactly, and one-hot encode `region` rather than passing `as.numeric(factor(region))`.

8. **Missing recent literature on Bayesian regression tree ensembles for causal mediation analysis.**  
   Although a central contribution claimed by the paper is using Bayesian Causal Forests to separate direct effects from mediating macroeconomic pathways, the mediation literature cited in Section 2.3 (`imai2010general`, `vanderweele2016mediation`, `acharya2016explaining`) stops in 2016 and omits the directly relevant modern literature on Bayesian tree ensembles for causal mediation analysis.  
   *Concrete fix:* Integrate and engage with the following recent methodological works in Section 2.3 and Section 4.2:
   - Linero, A. R. and Zhang, Q. (2025). Mediation analysis using Bayesian tree ensembles. *Psychological Methods*, 30(1), 60 - 82. DOI: [10.1037/met0000504](https://doi.org/10.1037/met0000504).
   - Ting, A. and Linero, A. R. (2025). Estimating heterogeneous causal mediation effects with Bayesian decision tree ensembles. *Journal of the American Statistical Association*, 1 - 14. DOI: [10.1080/01621459.2025.2491155](https://doi.org/10.1080/01621459.2025.2491155).
   - Linero, A. R. and Antonelli, J. L. (2023). The how and why of Bayesian nonparametric causal inference. *WIREs Computational Statistics*, 15(3), e1583. DOI: [10.1002/wcs.1583](https://doi.org/10.1002/wcs.1583).

### 3. Minor concerns

#### Figures

In a causal diagram (DAG), pre-treatment baseline context $X_{i,t}$ causes treatment assignment $Z_{i,t}$ ($2\text{A} \to 1$), not vice versa; treatment at wave $t$ cannot cause baseline covariates measured before wave $t$.
