# Referee Report: Line-by-Line Forensic Code, C++ Sampler, and Data Audit (Reviewer 5)

### 1. Overall impression

This paper investigates an important and policy-relevant question in public finance: whether government investment per tertiary (university) student improves a country's future tax take and shifts revenue structure toward direct income, profit, and capital gains taxation over 5-year and 10-year horizons once mediating macroeconomic changes are separated out. The conceptual framing around the UNESCO per-student spending denominator trap, the joint modelling of four disaggregated fiscal outcomes via a Multivariate Bayesian Causal Forest (`mvbcf_source_code.cpp`), and the longitudinal extension via a Three-Wave Longitudinal Bayesian Causal Forest (`lbcf_3wave.cpp`) are ambitious and well motivated. In addition, the conjugate multivariate Gaussian leaf updates (`update_mu`, `update_tau`), the integrated Gaussian marginal log-likelihood algebra (`log_lik`, `log_lik_tau`), and the conjugate Inverse-Wishart residual covariance update (`sample_sigma` with post-sampling standard-deviation rescaling) are algebraically well structured.

However, a line-by-line forensic audit of the R data pipeline (`src/r/01` to `06`), the two C++ MCMC engines (`src/cpp/mvbcf_source_code.cpp` and `src/cpp/lbcf_3wave.cpp`), and the exported CSV tables (`outputs/tables/`) reveals several severe methodological, algorithmic, and reproducibility flaws that must be fixed before this manuscript can be credibly released as a preprint or submitted to a journal. Most critically: (1) both C++ samplers omit the Metropolis-Hastings transition ratio $\log\bigl(q(T \mid T') / q(T' \mid T)\bigr)$ during tree `Grow` and `Prune` steps, violating detailed balance; (2) the three-wave longitudinal script (`src/r/05_run_longitudinal_bcf_tax.R`) feeds future 10-year mediators ($t \to t+2$) into the 5-year ($t \to t+1$) growth forest $\mu_1$, creating direct look-ahead leakage across waves while omitting two of the four claimed mediators and confounding baseline level residuals with trajectory changes; (3) the treatment regime thresholds (`Z_regime_inc`, `Z_real_spend_regime`, `Z_tert_gdp_regime`) are computed using full-sample 1985 - 2024 pooled medians and static end-of-sample (2024) World Bank income-group classifications applied retroactively to 1985; (4) the country-clustered Dirichlet weights post-multiply already-fitted i.i.d.-likelihood CATE draws without adjusting the leaf likelihood precision for within-country error correlation; (5) several benchmark and robustness implementations (`B1`, `R3a`/`R3b`, `R7`, `L6`, and `compute_ppc_metrics`) contain silent statistical bugs or sample-size mismatches; and (6) the repository lacks `run_all.sh`, any environment specification (`renv.lock`, `environment.yml`, or `DESCRIPTION`), and any unit tests in `tests/`, while hardcoding `/Users/andrewparnell/Documents/GitHub/education-tax-bcf` in every R script. My recommendation is **major revision** requiring re-execution of the C++ and R pipeline once the bugs detailed below are corrected.

### 2. Major concerns

1. **Omission of the Metropolis-Hastings transition ratio in C++ tree `Grow` and `Prune` proposals violates detailed balance (`src/cpp/mvbcf_source_code.cpp`, lines 770 - 788 and 838 - 859; `src/cpp/lbcf_3wave.cpp`, lines 714 - 733, 783 - 804, 855 - 876, 926 - 947, and 998 - 1019).**
   In both C++ samplers, tree structure updates (`Grow`, `Prune`, `Change`, `Swap`) are accepted with probability `a = exp(lnew - lold)`, where `log_lik(...)` and `log_lik_tau(...)` (`src/cpp/mvbcf_source_code.cpp`, lines 458 - 552; `src/cpp/lbcf_3wave.cpp`, lines 416 - 487) sum only the integrated Gaussian log-likelihood $\log P(R \mid T, \Sigma)$ and the tree structure prior $\log P(T)$ (`eq4p1` and `eq4p2`). They omit the Hastings proposal ratio $\log\bigl(q(T \mid T') / q(T' \mid T)\bigr)$. In the standard Bayesian Additive Regression Trees algorithm of Chipman, George, and McCulloch (1998, 2010), proposing a `Grow` move selects one of $b$ terminal nodes uniformly ($1/b$), a split predictor ($1/p$), and a split cutpoint ($1/n_u$), whereas the reverse `Prune` move selects one of $w_2$ singly-internal parent nodes (parents of two terminal leaves) uniformly ($1/w_2$). Even when the uniform predictor and cutpoint priors cancel with their proposal probabilities, the node-selection ratio $b / w_2$ (for `Grow`) and $w_2 / b'$ (for `Prune`) never cancels unless $b = w_2$. In addition, in `grow(...)` (`src/cpp/mvbcf_source_code.cpp`, lines 269 - 305; `src/cpp/lbcf_3wave.cpp`, lines 231 - 265), `node_vector[grow_index].variable` and `split_val` are overwritten on `proposal_tree` *before* checking `if (sum_more >= min_nodesize & sum_less >= min_nodesize)`.
   - *Concrete fix:* In both `src/cpp/mvbcf_source_code.cpp` and `src/cpp/lbcf_3wave.cpp`, include the exact log Hastings transition ratio $\log(b) - \log(w_2(T'))$ for `Grow` proposals and $\log(w_2(T)) - \log(b - 1)$ for `Prune` proposals in `lnew - lold`, and move the assignment of `variable` and `split_val` inside the `if (sum_more >= min_nodesize && sum_less >= min_nodesize)` block.

2. **Future look-ahead leakage, missing mediators, and baseline level confounding in the Three-Wave Longitudinal BCF script (`src/r/05_run_longitudinal_bcf_tax.R`, lines 75 - 125 and 184 - 202; `paper/paper.tex`, Appendix A, lines 480 - 485).**
   Auditing `src/r/05_run_longitudinal_bcf_tax.R` reveals three serious discrepancies between the longitudinal model implemented in code and the mathematical specification in Appendix A of `paper/paper.tex` (Equation 4):
   - *Future mediator leakage into Wave 1:* On lines 94 - 98 of `src/r/05_run_longitudinal_bcf_tax.R`, `growth_vars_med` combines baseline covariates with **both** 5-year ($t \to t+1$) and 10-year ($t \to t+2$) mediators (`"d_t1_log_gdp_pc_pct"`, `"d_t2_log_gdp_pc_pct"`, `"d_t1_tert_enrol_pp"`, `"d_t2_tert_enrol_pp"`). On lines 123 and 189 - 190, this identical matrix `X_growth_stack` is passed to `fast_lbcf3` as **both** `X_growth` (for the Wave 1 growth forest $\mu_1$ over $[t, t+1]$) and `X_growth2` (for the Wave 2 growth forest $\mu_2$ over $[t+1, t+2]$). Consequently, the Wave 1 prognostic forest $\mu_1$ splits on future economic growth and enrolment changes from years 5 to 10 (`d_t2_log_gdp_pc_pct` and `d_t2_tert_enrol_pp`) when predicting the year-5 outcome $y_1$.
   - *Omission of two mediators and baseline fiscal covariates:* Unlike `src/r/04_run_mvbcf_tax_models.R` (lines 129 - 130), `growth_vars_med` in `src/r/05_run_longitudinal_bcf_tax.R` omits the Gini inequality and life expectancy mediators (`d_t1_gini_disp`, `d_t2_gini_disp`, `d_t1_life_exp_yrs`, `d_t2_life_exp_yrs`). Moreover, `clean_mu_vars` (lines 75 - 92) drops all baseline fiscal variables (`non_resource_tax_gdp_pct`, `income_profit_tax_share_rev_pct`, `goods_services_tax_share_rev_pct`) across all four models rather than only dropping the baseline level $y_0$ of the target outcome, and `treat_vars` (lines 100 - 108) drops `income_profit_tax_share_rev_pct` (7 moderators instead of the 8 moderators used in Step 04).
   - *Unexplained baseline level variation leaking into $\mu_1, \tau_1$:* Because `y_vals <- c(spec$y0, spec$y1, spec$y2)` (line 184) stacks raw outcome levels across Waves 0, 1, and 2 while `y0` is excluded from `X_mu_stack`, $\mu_0(X_{i,t})$ cannot absorb country-specific baseline tax levels $y_{i,0}$. Any residual $y_{i,0} - \mu_0(X_{i,t})$ persists in $y_{i,1}$ and $y_{i,2}$ and can be absorbed by $\mu_1$ and $\tau_1$ (which have access to `pi_hat` and mediator variables not in `X_mu_stack`).
   - *Concrete fix:* Pass only $[t, t+1]$ mediators (`d_t1_log_gdp_pc_pct`, `d_t1_tert_enrol_pp`, `d_t1_gini_disp`, `d_t1_life_exp_yrs`) in `X_growth` for $\mu_1$, and $[t+1, t+2]$ incremental mediators in `X_growth2` for $\mu_2$; retain the non-target baseline tax structure variables in `clean_mu_vars` and `treat_vars`; and either model cumulative changes relative to baseline ($y_{i,w} - y_{i,0}$) or include country random/fixed intercepts so that baseline level residuals do not contaminate $\tau_1$ and $\tau_2$.

3. **Full-sample look-ahead leakage in treatment regime thresholds and retroactive application of static 2024 World Bank income groups (`src/r/02_build_clean_tax_panel.R`, lines 43 - 58, 226 - 236, and 260 - 283).**
   In `src/r/02_build_clean_tax_panel.R` (lines 228 - 236), the income-group medians (`med_spend_raw`, `med_real_spend`, `med_tert_gdp`, `med_edu_total`) used to define binary treatments (`Z_regime_inc`, `Z_real_spend_regime`, `Z_tert_gdp_regime`) are computed via `panel_df %>% group_by(income_group) %>% summarise(...)` **pooled across all 8 waves (1985 - 2024)**. Thus, whether a country-wave in `1985-1989` is assigned $Z_{i,t} = 1$ depends on future spending observations up to 2024. Furthermore, `income_group` in `data/raw/wb_countries_metadata.csv` (loaded on lines 43 - 58) is a static end-of-sample (2024) World Bank classification applied retroactively to all waves back to 1985. Countries that grew from low- or middle-income status in 1985 into high-income status by 2024 (such as South Korea or Chile) have their 1985 treatment assignment and baseline `income_group_num` covariate conditioned on their future 40-year economic trajectory.
   - *Concrete fix:* Compute regime medians within `(income_group, wave_index)` (or using an expanding historical window up to wave $t$ only), and assign wave-specific historical income-group tiers based on wave-$t$ real GDP per person (`gdp_per_capita_ppp_const2021`) rather than static 2024 classifications.

4. **Undisclosed heuristic formula for `non_resource_tax_gdp_pct` and false attribution to the UNU-WIDER Government Revenue Dataset (`src/r/02_build_clean_tax_panel.R`, lines 199 - 208 and 386 - 387; `paper/paper.tex`, lines 121 and 130).**
   Section 3.1 of `paper/paper.tex` (line 121) and `data_dictionary.csv` (`src/r/02_build_clean_tax_panel.R`, lines 386 - 387) state that domestic non-resource tax revenue is drawn from the UNU-WIDER Government Revenue Dataset (GRD). In reality, no UNU-WIDER GRD file exists in `data/raw/` or is read by any script. Instead, lines 200 - 204 of `src/r/02_build_clean_tax_panel.R` construct `non_resource_tax_gdp_pct` from World Bank WDI series using an undocumented piece-wise linear formula floored at 2.0% of GDP:
   `pmax(tax_rev_gdp_pct - 0.5 * pmax(res_rents_clean - 10.0, 0.0), 2.0)` when `tax_rev_gdp_pct` is observed, and falling back to `pmax(gov_rev_ex_grants_gdp_pct - 0.7 * res_rents_clean, 2.0)` when `tax_rev_gdp_pct` is `NA`. Then, lines 205 - 206 construct `inc_tax_gdp_pct` (Outcome 2) by multiplying `non_resource_tax_gdp_pct` (Outcome 1) by `income_profit_tax_share_rev_pct / 100` (Outcome 3), even though WDI's `GC.TAX.YPKG.ZS` is a percentage of *total revenue* (`GC.REV.XGRT.GD.ZS`), not non-resource tax revenue. This also makes Outcome 2 a deterministic product of Outcomes 1 and 3 in levels.
   - *Concrete fix:* Either integrate the actual UNU-WIDER GRD dataset into `data/raw/` with verified SHA-256 provenance, or remove the claim that UNU-WIDER GRD data are used and explicitly document the exact WDI resource-rent adjustment formula in Section 3.2 of `paper/paper.tex`.

5. **Post-hoc Dirichlet cluster weighting does not account for within-country error correlation, and MCMC chains are single-chain with low effective sample size (`src/r/04_run_mvbcf_tax_models.R`, lines 186, 235 - 240, and 261; `src/r/05_run_longitudinal_bcf_tax.R`, lines 163 - 178 and 215 - 217; `outputs/tables/tableA1_convergence_and_fit_diagnostics.csv`, line 14).**
   In `src/r/04_run_mvbcf_tax_models.R` (lines 235 - 240 and 261) and `src/r/05_run_longitudinal_bcf_tax.R` (lines 173 - 178 and 215 - 217), country-clustered inference is computed by multiplying the post-burn-in unit-level CATE matrix `tau_mat` from an i.i.d.-likelihood forest by Dirichlet weights $w^{(s)}_{c(i)}$ (`clu_draws <- colSums(tau_mat * res$obs_w)`). Because the leaf posterior precision in `mvbcf_source_code.cpp` (line 84, `arma::inv(sigma_tau) + njz % arma::inv(sigma)`) treats all $N$ country-waves as independent, and because strong shrinkage ($\alpha_\tau = 0.25, \beta_\tau = 3.0$) makes $\tau^{(s)}(W_{i,t})$ nearly constant across units $i$, reweighting $\tau^{(s)}(W_{i,t})$ across clusters after fitting leaves the posterior variance virtually unchanged (`clu_cri95` vs `ate_cri95` in `table1_mvbcf_ate_posteriors.csv` differ by $\le 0.02$ pp). Moreover, all models run only a **single MCMC chain** (`n_iter = 2000`, `n_burn = 750`), yielding an effective sample size of only `ESS = 58` for `M2b_Direct_10y` Outcome 4 (`tableA1_convergence_and_fit_diagnostics.csv`, line 14) and unadjusted Geweke $|z| > 3.0$ across multiple specifications in `table1_mvbcf_ate_posteriors.csv` (lines 2, 8, 9, 13, 14, 30, 33, 34, 36, 41).
   - *Concrete fix:* Run at least 4 independent parallel chains with $\ge 4,000$ iterations (2,000 burn-in) so that multi-chain split-$\hat{R} < 1.05$ and $\text{ESS} > 400$ across all outcomes, and clarify in Section 4.2 that the Dirichlet cluster bootstrap accounts for finite-sample cluster sampling uncertainty in $W_{i,t}$ rather than likelihood-level serial correlation in $\varepsilon_{i,t+h}$ (or include country random effects in the prognostic model).

6. **Flawed implementations in Ablation `B1`, Fixed-Effects Check `R3`, Measurement-Error Check `R7`, Leakage Audit `L6`, and PPC 95% Coverage (`src/r/04_run_mvbcf_tax_models.R`, lines 307, 476 - 482, 659 - 665, 685 - 692, and 767 - 770).**
   - *Ablation `B1` sample-size mismatch (lines 476 - 482):* Section 5.4 of `paper/paper.tex` (line 78 and Table 5 caption) claims that `B1` (Separate Univariate BCFs) is fitted on the **exact same dataset ($N = 283$, 112 countries)** as joint `mvbcf`.    - *Fixed-effects check `R3a`/`R3b` (lines 659 - 665):* `df_fe` demeans only the outcome variables `c(y5_cols, y10_cols)` within `iso3` across the full `df` (including waves where $Z_{i,t}$ is missing) without demeaning treatment $Z_{i,t}$ or covariates $X_{i,t}$. Subtracting $\bar{Y}_i$ from $Y_{i,t}$ while leaving $Z_{i,t}$ un-demeaned does not absorb country fixed effects correlated with treatment and biases $\hat{\tau}$ toward zero in countries with time-invariant $Z_{i,t} = 1$.
   - *SWIID measurement-error check `R7` (lines 685 - 692):* Whereas `paper/paper.tex` (line 196) describes `R7` as *"propagating Monte Carlo measurement error ($\text{SD} = 1.5$ Gini points)"*, lines 685 - 692 draw only a single random perturbation (`1` draw), use fallback `1.0` (not `1.5`), and perturb `gini_disp_harmonised` and `d_t2_gini_disp` independently instead of computing `d_t2_gini_disp` from the perturbed series.

Comparing `M2b_Direct_10y` (`table1_mvbcf_ate_posteriors.csv`, line 16: `ate_mean = 1.664`) against `R8_CountryBlocked_OOF_10y` (`table4_robustness_checks.csv`, line 36: `ate_mean = 1.437`) shows an actual difference of **`0.227` pp** on Direct Income Tax Share (nearly six times the claimed `0.04` pp bound).

Linearly adding the 95% half-width of the posterior mean $f_{i,j}$ and $1.96\bar{\sigma}_j$ (instead of adding variances in quadrature or drawing $y^{\text{rep},(s)}_{i,j} \sim \mathcal{N}(f^{(s)}_{i,j}, \sigma^{(s)2}_j)$ per MCMC draw) inflates the interval width and artificially pushes `ppc_cov95_pct` to `97.6% - 99.6%`.
   - *Concrete fix:* Filter `df` to the exact $N = 283$ four-outcome complete-case rows before running `B1`; demean both outcomes and covariates (or include country indicators/random intercepts) within the estimation sample for `R3`; compute `leakage_audit_tbl` dynamically *after* `R8` finishes; and compute PPC 95% intervals from posterior predictive draws $y^{\text{rep},(s)}_{i,j} = f^{(s)}_{i,j} + \varepsilon^{(s)}_{i,j}$.

### 3. Minor concerns

1. **Two conflicting definitions of `ESS` and `Geweke z` across scripts (`src/r/04_run_mvbcf_tax_models.R`, lines 164 - 183 vs `src/r/06_generate_figures_and_diagnostics.R`, lines 548 - 580).**
   `04_run_mvbcf_tax_models.R` computes `ess` using only the lag-1 autocorrelation (`n * (1 - r1) / (1 + r1)`) and computes `geweke_z` using i.i.d. variances (`var(x1) / n1`), whereas `06_generate_figures_and_diagnostics.R` sums autocorrelations up to lag 50 (`compute_ess`) and adjusts `compute_geweke_z` using `ess1` and `ess2`. Standardise on a single spectral/initial-positive-sequence estimator across all scripts.
