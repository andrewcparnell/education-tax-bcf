# Reviewer 8 Report: Author Pre-Submission Review (Andrew C. Parnell)

**Manuscript:** *Does Increasing Government Spending on Tertiary Education Improve the Future Tax Take? A Bayesian Causal Inference Analysis*  
**Recommendation:** Minor to Moderate Revision (21 Specific Action Items)  
**Date:** 10 November 2026

---

## Overall Assessment

The continuous-treatment Multivariate Bayesian Causal Forest (MV-BCF) framework, 5-fold country-blocked holdout validation, and empirical findings are solid, but several aspects of the exposition, figures, notation, and economic intuition need tightening before final submission. Below are 21 specific points that should be addressed across the manuscript and figures.

---

## Specific Comments (21 Items)

1. **Sentence length and clarity:** In general the writing should involve short, clear sentences that contain one fact.
2. **Paragraph length:** Paragraphs should not be less than 3 sentences, but should also not take up more than a third of a page. Four to six short sentences is ideal, though not a strict rule.
3. **Non-linearity and diminishing returns of university spending:** The abstract and text state: *"We find that each $+10$ percentage-point (pp) increase in per-student university spending as a share of GDP per person shifts..."* This sounds unrealistic if taken literally as an unbounded linear rule, because countries cannot keep increasing the amount they give to universities indefinitely. How do we model this non-linearly whilst still keeping the causal framework, or how do we account for diminishing returns (for example, through the $\mathbb{E}[Z \mid X]$ model, treatment-effect moderation $\tau(W)$, winsorisation, and our logarithmic specifications)?
4. **Remove the term "dose" from the paper:** Figure 1 and the text use the medical term "dose" (for example, *"1. Continuous University Dose (Four Ways of Measuring)"*). Remove the term "dose" from the paper and figures and use clear, natural policy language instead.
5. **Simplify Figure 1 headings and labels:** In Figure 1, *"1. Continuous University Dose (Four Ways of Measuring)"* could just be *"1. Four Ways of Measuring University Funding ($Z_1$ to $Z_4$)"* or similar. Review the other headings and items in Figure 1 for clarity and see if there is a simpler way to explain them.
6. **Smooth paragraph transition around Figure 1 in Section 1:** There is a paragraph that ends *"Figure 1 summarises our continuous-treatment study design for addressing all three obstacles across 121 countries from 1985 to 2024."* and then the next paragraph immediately starts with *"As illustrated in Figure 1..."* Move the first sentence to be the start of the next paragraph and re-phrase so that it links together neatly.
7. **Remove unexplained jargon from Figure 1:** Figure 1 includes the phrase `"Expected Dose E[Z | X] (RF)"` which will confuse readers at the start of the paper. What does that mean in this context, what is $Z$, why do we want $\mathbb{E}[Z \mid X]$, and what is RF? Replace it with plain English in Figure 1 and explain the concepts clearly when introduced in the text.
8. **Use explicit $Y_1, \dots, Y_4$, $Z_1, \dots, Z_4$, and $X$ notation in Figure 1 and the text:** It is slightly confusing that we have four tax outcomes and four ways of measuring university funding. Call the four tax outcomes $Y_1, \dots, Y_4$ and the four university funding measures $Z_1, \dots, Z_4$, and include $X$ in the middle boxes for the mediating and baseline control variables. Make clear that we model all four $Y$ variables simultaneously in every run, whereas we test the four $Z$ variables in turn across separate models.
9. **Expand the "first global study" contribution statement in Section 1:** The paragraph starting *"We believe this is the first global study to use modern causal inference techniques to test whether public university funding increases future tax revenue."* should be expanded to compare our approach directly with existing studies. The next paragraph can then contain the section-by-section outline of the paper.
10. **New paragraph for the author disclosure in Section 2.3:** The sentence starting *"Readers will note that the Multivariate Bayesian Causal Forest..."* should begin a new paragraph.
11. **Tree depth of the prognostic forest $\mu(\cdot)$ in Section 4:** We have a sentence stating *"To estimate the baseline trajectory $\mu(X_{i,t}, \hat{z}(X_{i,t}))$, we use an ensemble of 75 shallow decision trees (many having only one or two splits)."* Make clear that these are simply 75 decision trees and that whether they are shallow or deep is up to the model to learn from the data.
12. **Introduce the binary propensity score before contrasting it with continuous $Z$ in Section 4:** We have the sentence *"Because treatment $Z_{i,t} \in [0, 3.0]$ is continuous rather than binary, the binary propensity score $P(Z = 1 \mid X)$ no longer applies"*, without having mentioned the propensity score earlier. Precede this with a sentence explaining that in standard BCF, the treatment variable $Z$ is binary and a propensity score $P(Z = 1 \mid X)$ is used, and then explain how our continuous approach replaces it with $\hat{z}(X_{i,t}) = \mathbb{E}[Z_{i,t} \mid X_{i,t}]$.
13. **Regularisation priors on the $\tau(\cdot)$ trees in Section 4:** Instead of calling the $\tau$ ensemble *"30 shallower trees"*, state that we use 30 decision trees whose structures are chosen by the data and that, following Hahn et al. (2020), we place stronger regularisation priors on the $\tau$ tree shapes, with full hyperparameter details in Appendix A.
14. **Explain and test using $X$ for $\mu(\cdot)$ vs $W$ for $\tau(\cdot)$:** Explain clearly why we use the 19 covariates $X$ (plus four growth controls) for $\mu$ and 8 structural moderators $W \subset X$ for $\tau$, address whether one could argue for using the exact same variables in both parts of the model ($W = X$), and run a sensitivity check setting $W = X$.
15. **Remove Table 2 and clean up Figure 3 in Section 5.1:** Table 2 and Figure 3 say the same thing. Keep Figure 3 in the main paper, drop Table 2, and move the grey subtitle/note text in Figure 3 (explaining the credible intervals and $P$) into Figure 3's caption.
16. **New paragraph and cost-vs-return clarity for `"To put these continuous slopes..."` in Section 5.1:** Make `"To put these continuous slopes..."` a new paragraph with short, clear sentences. Clarify how the annual cost of a $+10\text{ pp}$ increase in funding compares to the billions of euro raised at Year 10, whether that $+10\text{ pp}$ funding commitment is given in one year or sustained across the five-year wave, and add a forward reference to the multi-cohort payback discussion in Section 6.
17. **Remove Table 3 and clean up Figure 4 in Section 5.2:** Figure 4 and Table 3 say the same thing. Keep Figure 4 in the main paper, drop Table 3, and move the extra grey text in Figure 4 into its caption.
18. **Streamline Section 5.4 (Model Fit and Holdout Validation):** Condense Section 5.4 to two short paragraphs on convergence and out-of-sample country validation with a single figure on the latter (`fig6_scaling_fit_and_holdout_validation.pdf`), moving the residual and holdout scatter plots to the Appendix and referencing them in the main paper.
19. **Show the $+5$-year negative point estimates (243 out of 371 country-waves, 65.5%) visually:** In Section 5.3 we note that 243 out of 371 country-waves (65.5%) have negative point estimates on total tax-to-GDP ($Y_1$) at $+5\text{ years}$. Plot these country-wave point estimates directly in Figure 6 (`fig5_longitudinal_country_trajectories.pdf`) and reference the figure in the text.
20. **Clarify Point 5 in Section 6.2 and include Ireland:** Point 5 in Section 6.2 was confusing because it looked as though all the examples given would cost more in university funding than they get back in tax. Include Ireland explicitly alongside New Zealand, and explain clearly why comparing a single year of tuition costs for an entire five-year student cohort against a single year of tax receipts at Year 10 (when only the first two graduate cohorts have entered the workforce out of an eight-wave, 40-year working career) makes the single-year cost look larger than the Year-10 return in high-enrolment countries.
21. **Add Dedication in Declarations and Statements:** Add a section titled **Dedication** reading: *"This paper is dedicated to the memory of Robert Christopher Parnell"*.
