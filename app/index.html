How This App Works & How to Use It

This tool lets policy makers explore how changes in government university funding affect a country's tax revenue over the following 5 to 10 years, based on historical data from 121 countries between 1985 and 2024.

Choosing Units (EUR, USD, GBP, or Percentage Points)

Use the Units menu in the top-right corner at any time to switch all estimates between EUR (€), USD ($), GBP (£) (shown as annual tax revenue per person, with total national amounts in billions or millions shown when you hover over any bar or point), or Percentage points (pp) of GDP / total tax revenue.

1. Future Tax Estimation

  • What it shows: The estimated change in annual tax revenue 5 or 10 years after a country increases its investment in university education.
  • How to use it: Pick a timeframe (5 years, 10 years, or compare both side by side), choose a tax measure, and tick any combination of countries on the left.
  • Reading the chart: The vertical dashed line marks zero (no effect). Points to the right of zero indicate an increase in tax revenue, with thick bars showing the 80% uncertainty range and thin bars showing the 95% uncertainty range.

2. Historical Data

  • What it shows: How university spending and tax revenues have actually moved over time across countries from 1990 to 2024.
  • How to use it: Press Play to animate the progression across five-year periods, or drag the time slider manually. Tick countries on the left (or use presets such as Big Movers, Long-Term Risers, or Long-Term Decliners) to trace their historical paths on the scatter plot and in the time-series charts below.

3. Full policy scenarios

  • What it shows: How the estimated tax impact for a single country changes if you either increase or reduce university funding while other economic or demographic characteristics also change.
  • How to use it: Select a country, choose whether to increase or reduce funding, and pick a 5-year or 10-year horizon. By default, all other country characteristics are set automatically to that country's observed average. Click Adjust other country factors to open the optional sliders and test custom scenarios.
