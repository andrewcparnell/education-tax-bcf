How This App Works & How to Use It

This tool lets policy makers, tax specialists, and education analysts explore how changes in government university funding affect a country's tax revenue over the following 5 to 10 years, based on historical data from 121 countries between 1985 and 2024.

Choosing Units (Country Total Currency, Per Person, or Percentage Points)

Use the Units menu in the top-right corner at any time to switch all estimates between Country Total Currency in EUR (€ billion / yr), USD ($ billion / yr), or GBP (£ billion / yr) (the default view, showing the estimated annual tax change across the whole country holding population and baseline GDP constant), Per-Person Currency (€, $, or £ per person per year), or Percentage points (pp) of GDP / total tax revenue.

1. Future Tax Estimation

  • What it shows: The estimated change in annual tax revenue 5 or 10 years after a country increases its per-student investment in university education.
  • How to use it: Pick a timeframe (5 years, 10 years, or 5 vs 10 years side by side), choose a tax measure, and tick any combination of countries on the left (or use peer-group pills such as EU & UK or Nordic vs Tuition).
  • Reading the chart: The vertical dashed line marks zero (no effect). Points to the right of zero indicate an increase in tax revenue, with thick bars showing the 80% uncertainty range and thin bars showing the 95% uncertainty range. Hover over any country to view its current university funding level, peer-group threshold status, baseline tax structure, and national currency total.

2. Historical Data

  • What it shows: How university spending (measured as % of GDP per person, real PPP dollars per student, university budget % of GDP, or enrolment %) and tax revenues have actually moved across countries from 1990 to 2024.
  • How to use it: Press Play to animate the progression across five-year periods, or drag the time slider manually. Click any bubble directly on the scatter plot (or use presets such as Big Movers or Denominator Trap) to highlight a country's historical path on both charts.

3. Full policy scenarios

  • What it shows: How the estimated tax impact for a single country splits between Tax Revenue Mobilisation (left panel: Income Tax and Total Tax per person) and Tax Mix Shift (right panel: Income Tax Share vs Goods/VAT Share of total tax).
  • How to use it: Select a country and click a one-click policy scenario (such as Boost Per-Student, Funded Expansion, Unfunded Crowding, or Budget Cut), or open Adjust other country factors to customise individual economic and demographic sliders.
