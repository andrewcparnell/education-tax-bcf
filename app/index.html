How This App Works & How to Use It

This tool lets policy makers, tax specialists, and education analysts explore how changes in government university funding affect a country's tax revenue over the following 5 to 10 years, based on historical data from 121 countries between 1985 and 2024.

Choosing Units (Country Total Currency, Per Person, or Percentage Points)

Use the Units menu in the top-right corner at any time to switch all estimates between Country Total Currency in EUR (€ billion / yr), USD ($ billion / yr), or GBP (£ billion / yr) (the default view, showing the estimated annual tax change across the whole country holding population and baseline GDP constant), Per-Person Currency (€, $, or £ per person per year), or Percentage points (pp) of GDP / total tax revenue.

1. Return on Investment (ROI)

  • What it shows: How the multi-year public cost of educating a single entering cohort of university students (Cohort Degree Input, marked by a gold diamond) compares against the cumulative additional tax revenue that cohort generates across a 40-year working career (or the single-year annual change after 5 or 10 years).
  • Why Cohort Input = 1 Year of System Cost: Because a university system holds all D year-groups of students simultaneously in any calendar year (e.g., 1st-, 2nd-, 3rd-, and 4th-year students), a single entering cohort makes up 1/D of the student body and studies for D years. Its total multi-year degree cost therefore equals one year of the system-wide +10 pp budget boost, while across a 40-year working career (eight 5-year waves) that cohort generates 8 × the Year-10 annual tax flow.
  • Should I look at Y1 or Y2? Focus on Y1 (Total Non-Resource Tax) for the bottom-line Exchequer return (capturing income tax, corporation tax, employer/employee payroll & social insurance, and VAT on graduate spending). Use Y2 (Direct Income & Profit Tax) as the conservative floor from paycheques and corporate profits alone, and Y3 to see how the tax mix shifts toward progressive direct taxation.

2. Historical Data (1985-2024)

  • What it shows: How university spending (measured as % of GDP per person, real PPP dollars per student, university budget % of GDP, or enrolment %) and tax revenues have actually moved across countries from 1990 to 2024.
  • How to use it: Press Play to animate the progression across five-year periods, or drag the time slider manually. Click any bubble directly on the scatter plot (or use presets such as Big Movers or Denominator Trap) to highlight a country's historical path on both charts.

3. Country Policy & Treasury Calculator

  • What it shows: A customisable country-by-country Treasury Return on Investment Brief (with 95% credible intervals, benefit-cost ratios, and probability of net positive return for both Y1 Full Exchequer Return and Y2 Direct Income-Tax Floor) alongside bar charts of Tax Revenue Mobilisation and Tax Mix Shift.
  • How to use it: Select any country, choose a funding measure (Z1 to Z4), funding change size, and one-click policy scenario (such as Boost Per-Student, Funded Expansion, Unfunded Crowding, or Budget Cut), or open Adjust other country factors to test counterfactual economic and demographic conditions.
